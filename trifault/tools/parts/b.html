[04:30–06:00] This is the Beyond Compilation study, Zhang et al., 2026. They gave a tool-augmented GPT-5.2 agent 227 graduate-level textbook statements to formalize. 87.2 percent compile: 198 of 227. Only 57.7 percent are faithful to the English: 131 of 227. So about 30 points of "it compiles" is not "it means the same thing". This is not one bad model. All 8 systems they tested have a gap, from 1.3 to 29.5 points. And this isn't judge noise: human review confirms at least 23.8 of those 29.5 points are real translation errors.

So you can't just pick one judge, human or model, and call it ground truth. You need something that weighs judges against each other and knows how far to trust each one. The game

Let's play.

says it can't tell doesn't say it

Hands up on each reveal.

TriFault · SF Lean Meetup
[17:00–18:00] Here's the game. I show you an English statement and the Lean an AI agent wrote for it. You vote three ways. Says it: the Lean means what the English means. Can't tell: the English is ambiguous or you can't decide. Doesn't say it: the Lean means something else. Hands up for each option when I ask. In the product you also tap the exact span of Lean that's wrong and pick a reason, so a vote carries where and why, not just a verdict. Four rounds: a warm-up, round 2, a bonus, and the boss.
