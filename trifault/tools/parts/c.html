TriFault · SF Lean MeetupRhee et al., Advanced Science, 2026
[31:00–32:30] How the engine works. A p-trit is a probabilistic trit: three states, 0, 1, 2, each with Boltzmann odds, P of z equals e to the minus E of z over the sum over u of e to the minus E of u. Change the energies and you change the odds. This isn't only math. A KAIST team, Rhee et al. in Advanced Science 2026, built NbOx p-trits and a ternary Potts machine out of them. The device: a titanium nitride via 150 nanometres wide, 20 nanometres of NbOx, and a 50-nanometre titanium nitride top electrode. The model on the right is that stack. The paper didn't report the substrate material or the via depth, so those parts of the model are not to scale. To be clear: we run p-trits in software today. We don't have hardware.

The engine

The verdict engine.

Every vote is a trit: 0 says it, 1 can't tell, 2 doesn't.

Every statement is a p-trit with energy

Ej(z) = −log πz − Σi log θi[z, vij]

θi is rater i's 3×3 confusion matrix. Block Gibbs alternates statements and raters.

It's Bayesian Dawid–Skene (1979) as p-trit physics.

TriFault · SF Lean MeetupDawid & Skene, 1979 · Rubric: Zhang et al., Beyond Compilation, 2026
[33:30–35:00] The verdict engine. Every vote is a trit: 0 means says it, 1 can't tell, 2 doesn't. Every statement j is a p-trit whose energy for state z is minus log of the prior pi z, minus the sum over raters i of log theta i at row z, column v i j, where v i j is rater i's vote on statement j. Theta i is rater i's 3 by 3 confusion matrix: how likely they are to vote each way given the true answer. Block Gibbs sampling alternates: sample every statement given the raters, then every rater's confusion matrix given the statements. Statisticians will recognise this: it's Bayesian Dawid–Skene from 1979, written as p-trit physics. Judge and human scores out of 10 become trits by the study's own rubric: 9 to 10 says it, 7 to 8 can't tell, 0 to 6 doesn't.
