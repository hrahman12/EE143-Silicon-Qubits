## How to use this pack

Rules the agent follows:
- Facts and numbers come only from the Key numbers table, each with its [ref] key.
- Figures come only from Figure sources. Publisher-copyright figures get redrawn, not pasted.
- Citations use the DOIs exactly as listed in the two paper tables.

## Key numbers (every fact the deck may use)

Compare fidelities with care: He2019 used natural Si and electron spins, while the 2025 processors use ²⁸Si and nuclear-spin registers.

| Topic | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Proposal | Kane's 1998 proposal uses ³¹P nuclear spins (I = 1/2) in Si as qubits | Kane1998; Schofield2025 §3.2 |
| Hilbert space | One P donor gives 4 states (2 electron × 2 nuclear); Sb (I = 7/2) gives 16 | Schofield2025 §1 |
| As vs P | As: I = 3/2, ionization energy 53.76 meV. P: I = 1/2, 45.59 meV | Stock2020 |
| Wavefunction | Donor electron Bohr radius of order 2 nm. Exchange depends strongly on exact donor position (valley oscillations) | Schofield2025 §3.2 |
| Coherence | Ion-implanted donors in ²⁸Si: coherence up to 35.6 s, single-qubit gates close to 99.99% | Schofield2025 §3.2 |
| Readout | SET charge sensor + spin-dependent tunnelling + on-chip microwave antenna (ESR/NMR) | Schofield2025 §3.2 |
| Lithography | Atom-wide lines at 3 V, 10 nA, 10 nm/s (10⁻² C/cm). 2 V and hundreds of nA reach 1000 nm/s | Schofield2025 §2.2 |
| Lithography | EUV writes at an equivalent 10¹⁴ nm/s, eleven orders of magnitude faster than STM lithography | Schofield2025 §2.2 |
| Lithography | Atomically precise mode: 10–20 nm/s, ≈100 atoms/s. Field-emission mode: ≈1000 atoms/s but no atomic precision | Schofield2025 §5.4 |
| Lithography | Placement precision ±1 atomic spacing | Schofield2025 §5.4 |
| Lithography | As single-atom windows written at 3.5 V, 3000 pA, 50 nm/s | Stock2024 |
| Surface prep | Acetone/IPA clean, outgas >8 h at 600 °C, repeated ≈1200 °C flash anneals | Stock2020; Stock2024 |
| Vacuum | STM base pressure <5×10⁻¹¹ mbar (Stock2020), 3×10⁻¹¹ mbar (Stock2024) | Stock2020; Stock2024 |
| Precursor | PH₃ steps PH₂+H → PH+2H → P+3H over minutes at room temperature | Stock2020 (citing Wilson2004) |
| Precursor | AsH₃ dissociates three orders of magnitude faster than PH₃. Isolated molecules fully dissociate in tens of seconds | Stock2020 |
| Precursor | As saturation coverage 0.24 ± 0.04 ML, independent of dose rate | Stock2020 |
| Dose | Single-atom arrays: AsH₃ at 5×10⁻⁹ mbar for 10 min | Stock2024 |
| Incorporation | Anneal at 350 °C (1–2 min), below the 380 °C onset of H₂ desorption, leaves the H resist intact. Each incorporated As ejects one Si atom, so the ejected Si counts the dopants | Stock2020; Stock2024 |
| Encapsulation | P: constant 250 °C overgrowth. Segregation ≈5 nm, full activation, mobility ≈10² cm²/V·s at 4.2 K | Oberbeck2002; Stock2020 |
| Encapsulation | As: unheated ultra-thin locking layer, then 500 °C × 10 s RTA, then constant-temperature epitaxy at ≈1 ML/min | Stock2020 |
| Encapsulation | Worked example: 2 nm Si unheated, 500 °C × 15 s, then 28 nm Si at 250 °C (layer buried 30 nm) | DAnna2023 |
| δ-layer | As: 1.6 ± 0.1 kΩ/□ and 28 ± 1 cm²/V·s at the plateau. 81% activation at 500 °C RTA; 1.45×10¹⁴ cm⁻² (88%) at 560 °C; ≈2 nm thick | Stock2020 |
| δ-layer | P layers typically reach 2.4×10¹⁴ cm⁻² | Stock2020 |
| δ-layer | ARPES: layers <1 nm thick at ≈10¹⁴ cm⁻². As confines better than P | Constantinou2023 |
| Contacts | Registration markers plus an STM–SEM system locate and contact buried devices | Ruess2004 |
| Contacts | Hall bars by optical lithography and RIE. Al deposited into etched holes through the δ-layer | DAnna2023 |
| Resist removal | Optimized one-step anneal removes the H resist without disturbing the P pattern | Hallam2005 |
| Inspection | X-ray fluorescence at ≈1 µm. After imaging: <0.2 Å As motion, <3% loss of active dopants. Activation 94 ± 5% | DAnna2023 |
| Placement | 2003: single P atoms with ≈1 nm accuracy; a P pair ≈12 nm apart on one dimer row | Schofield2003; Schofield2025 §4.1 |
| Placement | 2012: one P atom placed with one-lattice-site accuracy. Works at liquid-helium temperature; mK transport shows its discrete levels | Fuechsle2012 |
| Yield | P into an ideal 3-dimer window: 63 ± 10% (room-temperature dosing) | Ivie2021; Schofield2025 §4.1 |
| Yield | P in idealized 6-site windows: "less than 70%". Array yield 0.7ⁿ, so a 16-atom array works <0.4% of the time | Stock2024 |
| Yield | As in a 4-site window: 97 ± 2% single-atom yield, 1-in-4 lattice sites (≈0.6 nm²), 33% better precision than P | Stock2024 |
| Arrays | First defect-free 2×2 single-dopant array in Si. Iterative lithography/dose cycles could push single-atom yield toward 100%. A 4×4 array with non-ideal windows: 10–12 of 15 sites single, up to 80% | Stock2024; Schofield2025 §4.1 |
| Germanium | As from arsine incorporates into Ge(001) at room temperature, no anneal | Hofmann2023 |
| Ion implantation | Without ion counting, Poisson statistics give one ion per site only 36% of the time. Kane-type devices need ≈15 nm accuracy; sub-100 nm is routine | Schofield2025 §1, §2.4 |
| 2-qubit gate | ≈800 ps exchange gate between two P donor electrons. Readout 93.9 ± 1.3%, logical-basis gate fidelity 90 ± 3%. Donors 13.0 ± 0.5 nm apart, natural Si | He2019 |
| 3D device | 97.9% single-shot spin readout with <5 nm alignment between dopant layers | Koch2019 |
| 4-qubit processor | 3 P nuclei + 1 electron in ²⁸Si. Single-qubit 99.94–99.98%; CZ 99.32–99.65%; GHZ 96.2% | Thorvaldson2025 |
| 4-qubit processor | Grover search: ≈95% success (abstract). For the all-down (⇓⇓⇓) marked state: 93.46%, which is 98.87% of ideal | Thorvaldson2025 |
| 11-qubit processor | 4P register (4 nuclei + 1 electron) and 5P register (5 nuclei + 1 electron), linked by exchange (J ≈ 1.55 MHz), in ²⁸Si | Edlbauer2025 |
| 11-qubit processor | All gates 99.10–99.99%. Bell states 99.5% local, 97.0% across registers. CROT 99.64%. GHZ up to 8 nuclear spins | Edlbauer2025 |
| Simulator | 3×3 dopant-dot arrays tuned from metallic toward Mott-insulating behaviour | Wang2022 |
| Scaling | First H removal from Si(001):H with monochromatic EUV (109 eV). Industry EUV is 13.5 nm (≈92 eV). Secondary electrons drive it. Minutes of exposure with 300 W sources | Constantinou2024 |
| Scaling | EUV H desorption needs doses up to 18 000 J/cm². A 300 W source gives ≈6 W at the wafer, so several minutes per chip | Schofield2025 §5.3 |
| Scaling | Resistless EUV: 75 nm half-pitch, 31 nm tall SiO₂/Si gratings | Tseng2023 |
| Scaling | MEMS scanners could reach 10⁷ tips over a 300 mm wafer, still orders of magnitude too slow for CMOS volumes | Schofield2025 §2.2 |
| Scaling | Proposed hybrid: STM for qubit-scale features, EUV for larger arrays, gates and interconnects | Schofield2025 §5.3; Constantinou2024 |
